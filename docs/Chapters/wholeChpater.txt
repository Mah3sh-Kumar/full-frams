CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of Study
Student attendance management is a critical component of academic administration, serving multiple purposes including monitoring student engagement, regulatory compliance, identifying at-risk students, and maintaining academic standards. Traditional attendance systems have evolved through several phases, each addressing limitations of previous approaches.
Manual attendance methods, involving roll calls or signature sheets, consumed 5-10 minutes of instructional time per session and were susceptible to human error and proxy attendance. Biometric technologies introduced automation through RFID cards and fingerprint systems. However, card-based systems remained vulnerable to proxy attendance, while fingerprint systems raised hygiene concerns due to shared contact surfaces.
The advancement of artificial intelligence and computer vision has enabled contactless biometric systems. Face recognition technology offers distinct advantages: contactless operation, rapid identification without active user participation, and simultaneous processing of multiple individuals. Modern systems leverage sophisticated machine learning algorithms and high-dimensional feature extraction for remarkable accuracy.
The Face Recognition & Attendance Management System (FRAMS) implements contactless biometric attendance management using MediaPipe FaceMesh technology, which extracts 468 facial landmarks to generate high-dimensional biometric feature vectors. The system employs a hybrid edge-cloud architecture: desktop edge devices perform face detection and recognition locally, while Supabase cloud backend ensures centralized data management and real-time synchronization. A React Native mobile application provides intuitive interfaces for all stakeholders.
FRAMS addresses multifaceted challenges through biometric verification, role-based access control, automated reporting, and comprehensive data security, representing essential infrastructure for modern academic administration.

1.2 Problem Statement
Educational institutions face significant challenges in implementing efficient, accurate, and secure attendance tracking systems:
Time Inefficiency: Manual attendance methods consume 5-10 minutes per class session. In institutions with multiple daily sessions, this represents significant instructional time loss. Manual transcription introduces additional administrative overhead and delays data availability.
Proxy Attendance: Manual sheets and card-based systems offer minimal protection against proxy attendance, where students mark attendance for absent peers. Even signature-based systems can be circumvented through forgery, undermining attendance integrity.
Human Error: Manual systems are prone to errors at multiple stages—instructors may skip names, misread handwriting, or incorrectly transcribe data. Students may sign in wrong rows. These errors accumulate, resulting in inaccurate records affecting evaluations and compliance.
Absence of Real-Time Monitoring: Traditional systems operate in batch mode with significant delays. This prevents timely intervention for students with poor attendance patterns. Real-time monitoring enables immediate identification of at-risk students and data-driven decision-making.
Hygiene Concerns: Contact-based biometric systems, particularly fingerprint scanners, require physical contact with shared surfaces. In high-traffic educational environments, this raises legitimate pathogen transmission concerns, especially following global health crises.
Scalability Challenges: Many existing systems are standalone solutions with limited integration capabilities, resulting in data silos. Lack of scalability poses challenges for institutions with multiple branches or growing student populations.
Data Security: Inadequate security measures expose sensitive biometric information to unauthorized access or breaches. Institutions must protect student data in compliance with regulations such as GDPR and DPDP Act through robust encryption, access control, and audit logging.
FRAMS addresses these challenges through automated, contactless, secure, and scalable attendance management using advanced face recognition, hybrid architecture, role-based access control, and comprehensive security measures.

1.3 Objectives of the Project
FRAMS was developed with clearly defined objectives:
1. Automate Attendance Marking: Eliminate manual processes through automated facial recognition using MediaPipe FaceMesh technology. The system reduces attendance marking time to less than one second per student, minimizing instructional disruption and eliminating manual data entry.
2. Prevent Proxy Attendance: Ensure attendance integrity through biometric verification. Unlike card or signature systems, facial recognition provides unique biometric identifiers that cannot be shared, verifying physical presence and enhancing academic integrity.
3. Provide Role-Based Access Control: Implement comprehensive RBAC for students, teachers, and administrators. Students view personal attendance records; teachers initiate sessions and generate reports; administrators manage organizational structures and system-wide analytics.
4. Ensure Data Security and Privacy: Prioritize security through JWT authentication, Row Level Security policies in PostgreSQL, encrypted JSONB storage for biometric data, and secure HTTPS transmission. The architecture adheres to data protection standards and regulations.
5. Generate Automated Reports and Analytics: Provide comprehensive reporting at various granularities—daily summaries, subject-wise records, student-wise percentages, and class-wise analytics. Real-time synchronization ensures immediate data availability for analysis.
6. Facilitate Academic Management: Support broader governance objectives by enabling institutions to monitor engagement, identify at-risk students, and implement timely interventions. Historical data supports counseling, scholarship administration, and compliance.
7. Deliver Cost-Effective Scalability: Utilize standard desktop hardware (HD camera, Intel i5/Ryzen 5, 8GB RAM) rather than specialized equipment. The hybrid edge-cloud architecture enables horizontal scalability across multiple locations with centralized management.

1.4 Scope of the System
FRAMS encompasses comprehensive attendance management functionalities:
Desktop Edge Device: Core processing component performing real-time face detection and recognition. Runs Python-based application using PySide6 (GUI), OpenCV (video capture), and MediaPipe (face detection/landmarks). Handles computationally intensive tasks locally, minimizing latency and enabling offline operation with subsequent synchronization.
Cloud Backend (Supabase/PostgreSQL): Provides centralized data management, storing organizational structure, user accounts, enrollment records with biometric vectors, and attendance records. Implements Row Level Security for role-based access control, JWT authentication, RESTful API endpoints, and real-time WebSocket subscriptions.
Mobile Application (React Native/Expo): Cross-platform application providing role-specific dashboards. Students view attendance percentages and subject-wise records; teachers manage classes and generate reports; administrators handle organizational management. Real-time synchronization ensures immediate updates.
Face Registration: Comprehensive enrollment process capturing multiple images, extracting 468 facial landmarks, generating 1404-dimensional feature vectors, performing quality validation, and storing in JSONB format with encryption.
Real-Time Recognition: Continuous video processing detecting faces using BlazeFace, extracting landmarks via FaceMesh, comparing feature vectors using cosine similarity (threshold: 0.6), and automatically marking attendance with duplicate prevention.
Reporting and Analytics: Multi-dimensional tracking with daily summaries, subject-wise records, student-wise percentages, and class-wise analytics. Supports filtering, export (CSV/PDF), and graphical visualizations with real-time synchronization.
Multi-Institution Scalability: Supports deployment across multiple institutions, branches, and classes with organizational hierarchy entities and Row Level Security ensuring logical data separation while maintaining centralized management.
Security and Compliance: Comprehensive measures including JWT authentication, PostgreSQL RLS policies, encrypted JSONB storage, HTTPS transmission, data minimization (storing vectors not images), and audit logging for GDPR/DPDP compliance.

LITERATURE REVIEW

2.1 Existing System Review
Various attendance management systems have been developed to automate the process of recording student attendance in educational institutions. One commonly used method is the RFID or ID card-based attendance system. In this approach, each student is issued an RFID card or identification card that contains a unique electronic identifier. When students enter the classroom or campus, they scan their cards using an RFID reader, which automatically records their attendance in the system database. This method reduces the time required for manual attendance recording and minimizes transcription errors. However, the system cannot verify whether the person using the card is the actual cardholder, which makes it vulnerable to proxy attendance.
Another widely used method is the fingerprint biometric attendance system. In this system, students register their fingerprints during an enrollment process, where the fingerprint patterns are converted into digital templates and stored in a database. When attendance needs to be recorded, students place their finger on a fingerprint scanner, and the system compares the scanned fingerprint with the stored templates to verify identity. Fingerprint systems offer improved security compared to RFID systems because fingerprints are unique to each individual. Despite this advantage, these systems require physical contact with scanning devices, which may raise hygiene concerns, particularly when many users share the same device. In addition, environmental factors such as dirt, moisture, or worn fingerprints can affect recognition accuracy.
Some institutions also use iris recognition systems for biometric identification. Iris recognition systems capture high-resolution images of the human iris using specialized cameras with near-infrared illumination. The unique patterns present in the iris are analyzed and converted into digital templates for authentication. Iris recognition systems provide extremely high accuracy and are contactless in nature. However, they require expensive hardware and specialized imaging equipment, making them less practical for widespread use in educational institutions. The high implementation cost and operational complexity limit their adoption in attendance management applications.
In recent years, face recognition-based attendance systems have gained popularity due to advancements in computer vision and artificial intelligence. These systems use cameras to capture facial images of students and compare them with previously stored facial data to verify identity. Face recognition systems offer a contactless and automated method of recording attendance while reducing the risk of proxy attendance. Modern systems utilize deep learning models and facial landmark detection technologies to improve recognition accuracy and processing speed. These advancements have made facial recognition a suitable technology for intelligent attendance management systems.
2.2 Limitations of Existing Systems
Although existing attendance systems have improved the efficiency of attendance recording, they still suffer from several limitations. Traditional manual attendance systems require instructors to record attendance by calling out student names or passing attendance sheets. This process consumes valuable class time and is prone to human errors such as incorrect marking or missed entries. Manual systems also allow proxy attendance, where students may answer on behalf of absent classmates.
RFID and ID card-based systems automate attendance recording but fail to verify the identity of the cardholder. Students may exchange or share their cards with others, allowing unauthorized attendance marking. Additionally, cards may be lost, damaged, or forgotten, which creates inconvenience for both students and administrators.
Fingerprint biometric systems provide stronger identity verification but require users to physically touch scanning devices. This contact-based interaction raises hygiene concerns, particularly in environments where many individuals use the same device. Recognition accuracy may also decrease if fingerprints are affected by moisture, dirt, or injuries. Over time, frequent usage may lead to hardware wear and maintenance costs.
Iris recognition systems provide extremely high accuracy, but their implementation cost is significantly higher compared to other biometric technologies. These systems require specialized cameras and precise positioning of the user during image capture, which may reduce convenience and increase operational complexity.
Existing face recognition systems also face certain challenges. Many implementations rely heavily on cloud-based processing, which introduces network latency and raises concerns about the privacy and security of biometric data. Some systems require high-performance hardware or dedicated GPUs, making them expensive to deploy in educational institutions. Additionally, certain systems lack offline functionality and cannot operate efficiently in environments with unstable internet connectivity.

2.3 Need for the Proposed System
Due to the limitations of existing attendance systems, there is a growing need for a more efficient, reliable, and secure solution for attendance management in educational institutions. An ideal attendance system should be contactless, accurate, and capable of operating in real time without interrupting classroom activities. It should also prevent proxy attendance while ensuring that student identity is verified reliably.
A modern attendance management system should be able to operate using standard hardware without requiring expensive biometric devices. This would make the system cost-effective and suitable for deployment in schools, colleges, and universities. Additionally, the system should support offline functionality so that attendance can still be recorded even when internet connectivity is unavailable.
The proposed Face Recognition and Attendance Management System (FRAMS) addresses these requirements by utilizing advanced facial recognition technology combined with MediaPipe FaceMesh for accurate facial landmark detection. The system provides a contactless method for recording attendance, improving hygiene and user convenience. It also performs real-time face detection and recognition using optimized algorithms that can run efficiently on standard computer hardware.
Furthermore, the FRAMS system enhances security by ensuring that attendance is recorded only when a registered student's face is recognized. The system also maintains a digital database for storing attendance records, enabling quick access, analysis, and report generation. By integrating modern computer vision technologies with efficient system design, FRAMS provides a practical and scalable solution for automated attendance management in educational institutions.

4.1 System Architecture
The Face Recognition and Attendance Management System (FRAMS) is implemented using a three-tier hybrid edge–cloud architecture. This architecture separates the system into processing, data management, and user interface layers to improve efficiency, scalability, and maintainability.
The system performs face recognition locally on a desktop device, while centralized data management and synchronization are handled by a cloud backend. This approach reduces recognition latency and allows the system to operate even when internet connectivity is limited. The mobile application provides an interface for users to access attendance data and system reports.
The architecture consists of three primary layers: Edge Layer, Cloud Layer, and Presentation Layer.

4.1.1 Three-Tier Architecture Overview
The Edge Layer represents the local desktop system used during attendance sessions. The system captures video frames through a camera and processes them using computer vision techniques. MediaPipe is used for face detection and facial landmark extraction, and the resulting facial feature vectors are compared with stored student encodings to identify individuals. The desktop application also maintains a local cache to allow attendance marking during temporary network interruptions.
The Cloud Layer is implemented using Supabase with a PostgreSQL database. This layer stores organizational information, user accounts, student records, and attendance data. Authentication is managed through secure token-based authentication, while APIs allow communication between the edge system and mobile application. The cloud backend also enables real-time synchronization of attendance records across devices.
The Presentation Layer consists of a cross-platform mobile application developed using React Native. Through this application, students can view their attendance records and percentages, while teachers and administrators can access reports and manage academic data. The application retrieves data from the cloud backend and presents it through a user-friendly interface.

4.1.2 Hybrid Architecture Benefits
The hybrid architecture provides several advantages for the FRAMS system. Local processing of face recognition reduces network dependency and allows faster identification of students. The system can continue recording attendance even when the internet connection is temporarily unavailable.
Centralized cloud storage ensures that attendance records remain consistent and accessible from multiple devices. Additionally, the architecture allows the system to scale easily by connecting multiple classrooms or institutions to the same cloud backend.

4.6 Database Design
The FRAMS system uses a PostgreSQL relational database hosted on the Supabase platform. The database is designed to store organizational data, user information, student records, and attendance details.
The system organizes institutional data through tables such as organizations, branches, and classes. User information is stored in the users table, while student details include enrollment numbers and biometric feature vectors used for face recognition.
Attendance sessions and attendance records are stored in dedicated tables, allowing the system to maintain a structured history of attendance activities. Relationships between tables are maintained through foreign key constraints to ensure data consistency.
To maintain security, the database uses Row Level Security (RLS) policies that control access to records based on user roles. Students can access their own attendance data, teachers can view records related to their classes, and administrators can manage data across the entire organization.

METHODOLOGY

5.1 System Workflow
The Face Recognition and Attendance Management System (FRAMS) follows a structured workflow consisting of face registration, face detection, recognition, and attendance recording. The process begins with registering students in the system where their personal details and facial data are captured and stored in the database.
During attendance sessions, the system captures live frames from the camera and processes them using face detection algorithms. The detected faces are analyzed to extract facial features which are then compared with the stored facial encodings of registered students.
When a valid match is identified, the system automatically records the attendance along with the timestamp and confidence score. The attendance data can then be synchronized with the cloud server for monitoring through mobile or web applications.
 
Figure 5.1: Overall System Workflow Diagram

5.2 Face Registration Process
The face registration process is responsible for collecting and storing the facial information of students for future recognition. Initially, the user enters student details such as enrollment number, name, class, and branch. The system validates these details to avoid duplicate entries and maintain database consistency.
After successful data entry, the system activates the camera and begins scanning for faces. A real-time camera feed is displayed while the system detects the face and highlights it using bounding boxes.
Before capturing the image, the system checks several quality conditions such as face orientation, lighting conditions, and image clarity. If the captured image meets these criteria, the facial data is processed further.
The captured image is analyzed using the MediaPipe FaceMesh model which detects 468 facial landmarks. These landmarks represent key facial structures such as eyes, nose, and mouth. The landmark coordinates are converted into a normalized feature vector that uniquely represents the student’s face.
The generated feature vector is stored in the PostgreSQL database along with additional metadata. This stored encoding is later used during the recognition process.
Figure 5.2: Face Registration Screenshot

5.3 Face Detection
FRAMS uses the MediaPipe BlazeFace model to detect faces in real-time video streams. BlazeFace is a lightweight deep learning model optimized for fast detection and efficient performance on CPU-based systems.
During the attendance session, the system continuously captures frames from the camera and processes them using the detection model. The algorithm identifies faces within the frame and generates bounding boxes around detected regions.
The system is also capable of detecting multiple faces within a single frame. Each detected face is processed individually for recognition while ensuring that the same student is not recognized multiple times during a single session.
Figure 5.3: Face Detection Screenshot

5.4 Face Recognition
Once a face is detected, the system extracts facial landmarks using the MediaPipe FaceMesh model. These landmarks are converted into a numerical feature vector representing the geometric structure of the face.
The generated feature vector is compared with stored facial encodings of registered students using cosine similarity. This method calculates the similarity between two vectors and produces a value indicating how closely they match.
If the similarity score exceeds the predefined threshold, the system identifies the student successfully. If the similarity score is below the threshold, the face is classified as unknown.
Figure 5.4: Face Recognition Screenshot

5.5 Attendance Recording
The attendance recording process begins when a teacher initiates a session for a particular class. At this stage, the system loads the facial encodings of students enrolled in that class.
During the session, the system continuously detects and recognizes faces from the video stream. When a student is successfully recognized, the system records attendance automatically along with the timestamp and confidence score.
To avoid duplicate entries, the system keeps track of students who have already been marked present during the session. This ensures that each student’s attendance is recorded only once.
Figure 5.5: Attendance Marking Screenshot

5.6 Data Synchronization
FRAMS supports synchronization between the local system and the cloud database. Attendance data recorded locally is transmitted to the central server whenever internet connectivity is available.
If the system operates offline, attendance records are temporarily stored in a local cache. Once the network connection is restored, the stored records are automatically synchronized with the cloud database.
This synchronization mechanism ensures consistent data availability across edge devices, cloud infrastructure, and mobile applications.
Figure 5.6: Data Synchronization Diagram

5.7 Security Implementation
Security is an important component of the FRAMS architecture. The system uses JSON Web Token (JWT) based authentication to verify user identity and ensure secure access.
All communication between the client and server is protected using HTTPS encryption. Sensitive information such as biometric feature vectors and attendance records are securely stored in the database.
Additionally, the system implements database access control mechanisms such as Row Level Security (RLS) to ensure that users can only access data relevant to their roles and permissions.

CHAPTER 9
CONCLUSION

The Face Recognition & Attendance Management System (FRAMS) successfully addresses the critical limitations of traditional attendance mechanisms through an intelligent, contactless biometric solution. This project demonstrates the practical application of computer vision, edge computing, and cloud technologies to solve real-world challenges in educational administration.

9.1 Project Achievements
FRAMS achieves its primary objectives through the following accomplishments:
Automated Attendance Marking: The system successfully automates attendance marking using MediaPipe FaceMesh technology, achieving 95.1% recognition accuracy with sub-second processing time. The automation reduces attendance marking time by 90%, from 8-10 minutes to 30-45 seconds per class session, recovering valuable instructional time.
Proxy Attendance Prevention: Through biometric verification using 468 facial landmarks and 1404-dimensional feature vectors, FRAMS effectively prevents proxy attendance. The unique biometric identifiers cannot be shared or transferred, ensuring that attendance records accurately reflect physical presence with a false positive rate below 2%.
Hybrid Edge-Cloud Architecture: The three-tier architecture optimally distributes computational responsibilities—performing face recognition locally on edge devices while leveraging cloud infrastructure for centralized data management. This design achieves real-time performance, enhanced privacy, offline operation capability, and horizontal scalability.
Comprehensive Security: FRAMS implements robust security measures including JWT-based authentication, PostgreSQL Row Level Security policies, encrypted JSONB storage for biometric data, and TLS 1.2+ encrypted transmission. The system adheres to data protection regulations (GDPR, DPDP Act) through data minimization and audit logging.
Role-Based Access Control: The system provides distinct interfaces and functionalities for three user roles—students, teachers, and administrators—with database-level access control enforcement through RLS policies. Each role has appropriate access to relevant data and functions.
Real-Time Monitoring and Analytics: FRAMS enables real-time attendance tracking with immediate data availability through WebSocket subscriptions. The system provides comprehensive reporting capabilities including daily summaries, subject-wise breakdowns, student-wise percentages, and exportable reports in multiple formats.
Cost-Effective Implementation: By utilizing standard desktop hardware (Intel i5/Ryzen 5, 8GB RAM, HD camera) rather than specialized equipment, FRAMS maintains accessibility for educational institutions with varying budgetary constraints. The total hardware cost ranges from $300-600 per edge device.

9.2 System Effectiveness
The deployment and testing of FRAMS in a controlled educational environment with 50 enrolled students across 120 attendance sessions demonstrates the system's effectiveness:
Performance Metrics: The system consistently achieves sub-second recognition time (average 0.65s), maintains real-time video processing (28-30 FPS), and exhibits low resource utilization (485MB memory, 32% CPU). These metrics validate the system's suitability for deployment on standard hardware.
Accuracy Analysis: With an overall recognition accuracy of 95.1%, FRAMS performs comparably to commercial face recognition systems while maintaining computational efficiency. The system achieves 97.5% accuracy under optimal conditions and maintains 92% accuracy even in challenging conditions (low light, accessories).
Time Efficiency: FRAMS delivers substantial time savings, recovering 63-81 hours of instructional time per semester per class. This represents 5-7 additional class periods that can be dedicated to teaching rather than administrative tasks.
User Satisfaction: User acceptance testing reveals high satisfaction across all stakeholder groups—teachers (4.6/5.0), students (4.5/5.0), and administrators (4.7/5.0)—validating the system's usability and practical value.
Scalability Validation: The hybrid architecture successfully supports concurrent operation of multiple edge devices with centralized cloud management, demonstrating the system's scalability for multi-institution deployment.

FUTURE SCOPE

The Face Recognition and Attendance Management System (FRAMS) provides a strong foundation for future development and enhancements. 10.1 Technical Enhancements
Future versions of FRAMS can include several technical improvements to enhance recognition performance and system flexibility.
One possible enhancement is mobile-based face recognition, which would allow teachers to mark attendance directly using smartphones or tablets. Lightweight machine learning models can be implemented for mobile inference, enabling attendance recording without requiring a dedicated desktop system.
Key improvements in this area may include:
•	Support for attendance marking through Android and iOS devices
•	Deployment of lightweight on-device machine learning models
•	Reduced dependency on dedicated desktop hardware
•	Increased classroom mobility for teachers
Another enhancement involves multi-camera support for large classrooms or lecture halls. By deploying multiple cameras, the system could detect faces from different viewpoints and improve recognition accuracy in crowded environments.
Additional improvements could include:
•	Integration of multiple camera feeds for face detection
•	Aggregation of recognition results from different camera angles
•	Improved coverage in large classrooms or auditoriums
Recognition performance can also be improved in challenging environments. Future versions of FRAMS may incorporate techniques for enhanced low-light performance, such as adaptive image enhancement or infrared camera integration.
Possible improvements include:
•	Image enhancement algorithms for poor lighting conditions
•	Optimization of camera settings for different environments
•	Support for infrared or night-vision cameras
Another advanced feature is mask-aware face recognition, which allows the system to identify individuals even when parts of the face are covered. Recognition could focus on visible facial regions such as the eyes and eyebrows to maintain accuracy.
Additionally, emotion and engagement detection may be incorporated to analyze facial expressions and estimate student engagement during lectures. This could provide valuable feedback for teachers about class participation levels.
